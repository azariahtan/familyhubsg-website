How to check what insurance policies you have in Singapore

Last checked 24 November 2026 · 5 minute read

No single place shows every policy you hold. A government service covers part of it. For the rest, you ask the insurers. This guide covers your own policies first, then a parent's or relative's after they have died.

The short version

  1. Your life and health policies: SGFinDex shows those from seven insurers.
  2. Your hospital cover: the CPF Healthcare dashboard.
  3. Everything else, and a family member's policies: ask the insurers directly.

Check your own policies

1. SGFinDex

SGFinDex lets you view your policy details from the participating insurers in one place. MAS says it is currently free to use. Singpass is only the login: your policies are shown in the app or website of a participating bank or insurer.

  1. Open the app or website of a participating bank or insurer and log in.
  2. Choose SGFinDex and log in with Singpass.
  3. Pick the insurers you want to connect. You then log in to each one to give your consent. AIA Singapore, Income, Prudential Singapore and Singlife let you do this with Singpass. The others use their own login, so you need an online account with them.
  4. Go back and retrieve your data.

What SGFinDex will not show

  • Policies you do not own. It only returns policies where you are the policyholder or a joint policyholder. A policy a parent owns on your life will not appear.
  • Policies that are no longer active.
  • Other insurers. The seven are AIA Singapore, Great Eastern, HSBC Life, Income, Manulife Singapore, Prudential Singapore and Singlife.
  • Other kinds of cover. Its insurance data is for individual life and health policies. Motor, home and travel insurance are not included.
  • Today's picture. It shows month-end details. HSBC Life updates daily. The other insurers update by the 7th day after month-end.

2. CPF Healthcare dashboard

Log in at cpf.gov.sg/healthcare and open "Coverage and premium details". MOH says this shows MediShield Life and Integrated Shield Plan coverage and premiums for you and for the dependants whose premiums you pay.

3. The insurer, or your own papers

For a policy that SGFinDex does not show, ask the insurer or log in to its app. The Life Insurance Association (LIA) lists its member life insurers. The General Insurance Association (GIA) lists general insurers, such as motor, home and travel.

Our suggestion, not an official step: look through old emails and bank or card statements for regular payments to an insurer you do not recognise, and ask your agent or adviser for a list of everything you hold with them.

Find a parent's or relative's policies after they have died

Older guides point you to LIA's online register of unclaimed insurance proceeds. LIA's page now says that register has been permanently removed.

LIA says that if you want to know whether a family member had policies, you contact the insurance companies directly.

  1. List the likely insurers. Our suggestion: check their papers, emails, bank statements and will, and ask their agent or adviser if they had one.
  2. Contact each insurer. Use the LIA and GIA lists above. Each insurer has its own process. LifeSG says to ask what documents it needs, such as a death certificate and proof of your relationship to the person.
  3. If they were a CPF member, check CPF. If they were covered under the Dependants' Protection Scheme (DPS), CPF informs the insurer, Great Eastern Life, which then sends claim details to the DPS nominee or the member's address. MediShield Life and Integrated Shield Plan cover ends on death.
  4. Once you find a policy, ask about the claim. LIA says a Grant of Probate or Letters of Administration may be needed before a payout if there is no valid nomination. For legal questions, speak to a lawyer.

Once you have found them

Write each policy in one list, with the same details every time. Our guide How to keep track of all your family's insurance policies shows what to record. Do it while you are well, so your family does not have to search later.

Common questions

Is SGFinDex safe?

MAS says your data is shared only with your consent, and is encrypted when it is retrieved. SGFinDex itself cannot read or store it. Your consent lasts one year, and it is extended when you retrieve your data again, if the institution's system supports that.

Why is one of my policies missing from SGFinDex?

The usual reasons: you are not the policyholder (for example, a parent owns a policy on your life), the policy is no longer active, the insurer does not take part, the policy is not a life or health policy, or the insurer has not yet sent its month-end update. Ask the insurer directly.

Can I use SGFinDex to find a parent's policies?

The official pages describe SGFinDex as a way to retrieve your own information with your own Singpass. For a family member's policies, LIA says to contact the insurance companies directly.

How do I check my insurance in Singpass?

Singpass is only the login. You open SGFinDex from the app or website of a participating bank or insurer, log in with Singpass, and your policies are shown there.
