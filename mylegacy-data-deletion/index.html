FamilyHub SG / My Legacy changes

My Legacy will delete your stored data on 4 January 2027. Here’s what to save and where to keep it.

From 4 January 2027, MyLegacy@LifeSG permanently deletes the personal information, funeral plans and will records that users entered themselves. Your Advance Care Plan, CPF nomination and Lasting Power of Attorney are not affected. If you want to keep anything you typed in, download it before then. Anyone you shared it with as a Trusted Person loses access on the same day.

Last reviewed 21 October 2026 against the official LifeSG notice and its help answers. This page is general information, not legal or financial advice.

Deadline: 4 January 2027 Singapore time. After this date the records are permanently deleted, with no access for you or your Trusted Persons, according to the official notice.

What is deleted, and what stays

My Legacy is not shutting down. It is stopping storage of the information you type in yourself, and keeping the records it pulls from government sources.

Deleted on 4 January 2027

  • Personal information: important documents, financial accounts, bills and payments, properties, vehicles and digital accounts
  • Funeral plans
  • Will records. You can no longer store or update will details in My Legacy during the transition
  • Your Trusted Persons lose access to all of the above

Not affected

  • Advance Care Plan
  • CPF nomination
  • Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA)
  • These keep syncing to your account from government records, and Trusted Persons you shared them with keep access

LifeSG’s stated reason is that information people maintain themselves drifts out of date, and My Legacy became one more place to keep updated. That problem does not go away when you move your information somewhere else. Whatever you choose, set a yearly reminder to check it.

What to do before 4 January 2027

  1. Download your summaries

    Log in to MyLegacy@LifeSG with Singpass and open each section you used: personal information, will and funeral plan. LifeSG makes one summary document for each section. Download each one.

    No download option in a section? LifeSG says that means nothing is stored there. If you are sure you had something there, contact LifeSG.

    The summary says whether you uploaded supporting documents, but does not contain them. Download those separately from each section. You can no longer edit before you download, so fix anything in the file afterwards. Open each file to check it works.

  2. Decide where each type of information will live

    Use the worksheet below. One place per type of information is easier to keep current than five.

  3. Tell the people who need to know

    If you named Trusted Persons, tell them the old access ends on 4 January 2027 and show them where things are now. If someone named you, ask them to do the same for you.

Your will record

My Legacy is moving away from holding will details itself. During the transition you cannot store or update will details there, and existing will records are deleted on 4 January 2027. The official notice suggests you may consider depositing a record with the Singapore Academy of Law (SAL) Wills Registry. LifeSG says it is working towards retrieving will-storage information from that registry through My Legacy.

  • It is optional. The registry is non-mandatory and confidential.
  • It does not store your will. SAL says it does not keep the will or a copy. It records your details as the person making the will, the date of the will, who drew it up, and where it is held.
  • Fees: SAL’s site lists $50 per will deposit submission and $10 per search.
  • Your will is valid either way. LifeSG says its validity does not depend on depositing a record, and that the registry keeps the information for 120 years from the testator’s date of birth.

The original will still needs a safe, known place, and your executor should know where it is. For questions about your own will, speak to a lawyer. This page cannot advise on that.

If you are someone’s Trusted Person

After 4 January 2027 you will no longer have access to the affected information. The official advice is to speak to the person who shared it with you about how they want to keep you informed.

  • Ask them for a copy of what matters to you, or a short list of where things are.
  • Check that you can open whatever they give you.
  • Their Advance Care Plan, CPF nomination and LPA are not affected, and your access to those stays.

Where to keep it instead

Keep one list that says where each thing is, and give each type of information one home. Our guide to organising important family documents shows how. Do not put passwords or full account numbers on the list.

Using FamilyHub SG for this

FamilyHub SG is a household records app for Singapore families, built and run by the founder with one IT developer. It is in a free beta and is not affiliated with LifeSG, My Legacy or any government agency. Here is what it does and does not do for this use.

What it does

  • Keeps records for properties, loans, insurance, investments, savings and CPF or SRS accounts, credit cards, other assets (including vehicles) and a home inventory, each tagged to a family member.
  • Lets you attach PDFs or photos (up to 10 MB each) or save a link to where a document lives. Free households share 10 MB in total.
  • Has an Estate Planning checklist for your Will, LPA, CPF nomination, Advance Medical Directive and Advance Care Plan, with notes such as where the physical copy is kept.
  • Lets you invite family as a Member (can edit) or a Viewer (can only look), and export everything to Excel, a full backup .zip or a Word summary.

What it does not do

  • It does not import from My Legacy. You enter your information yourself.
  • It does not connect to Singpass, your bank, your insurers or any government system, including the CPF Board and the Wills Registry.
  • It has no section for digital accounts or funeral plans. Use the Notes on a record.
  • There is no “release only after I pass away” setting. People you invite can see what their role allows straight away.
  • It is not a will and not legal advice. It is new, so keep your own copy of anything important.

How your data is handled

Connections use HTTPS and stored data is encrypted. The database and uploaded files are hosted in Singapore, and each household’s data is separated by access rules. It is not end-to-end encrypted, and the founder and an IT developer can technically access stored data to run the service. The privacy policy has the details.

How to move your information across

  1. Open app.familyhubsg.com, sign in and add the family members.
  2. Add the records from your worksheet. Attach scans or save links, and use Notes for where originals are kept.
  3. Tick off your Estate Planning items and invite your family as Members or Viewers.
  4. Download the full backup from Settings → Data and keep a copy somewhere else.

Try it with a few records first

FamilyHub SG is free during the beta. Start with one property or one policy and see whether it suits how your household works. Use made-up numbers if you prefer.

Questions and answers

Is My Legacy shutting down?

No. It stops storing what users entered themselves, but the dashboard keeps showing government-sourced records such as your Advance Care Plan, CPF nomination and LPA.

Will my CPF nomination, LPA or Advance Care Plan be deleted?

No. LifeSG says they are not affected, and Trusted Persons you shared them with keep access.

What happens if I do nothing before 4 January 2027?

Your stored personal information, will records and funeral plans are permanently deleted, and neither you nor your Trusted Persons can access them.

How do I download my information from My Legacy?

Log in to MyLegacy@LifeSG with Singpass. Open each section you used (personal information, will, funeral plan) and download its summary document. Download any supporting documents separately. Do it before 4 January 2027. If a section shows no download option, LifeSG says nothing is stored there.

Can I recover my records after 4 January 2027?

LifeSG says the records are permanently deleted and no longer accessible to you or your Trusted Persons. Its help pages do not describe any way to recover them afterwards. If you have a problem before the date, use “Contact us” on the LifeSG help site.

Where is My Legacy vault?

LifeSG says it has been renamed the dashboard. Log in to MyLegacy@LifeSG with Singpass to reach it.

Can I still store or update my will in My Legacy?

No. During the transition you cannot store or update will details there. LifeSG suggests considering the SAL Wills Registry.

What does the SAL Wills Registry cost, and does it keep my will?

SAL’s site lists $50 per will deposit submission and $10 per search. It does not keep the will or a copy, and using it is not mandatory.

I am a Trusted Person. What changes for me?

You lose access to the affected information after 4 January 2027. Ask the person who shared it how they want to keep you informed.

Is it safe to put my financial details in an app like FamilyHub SG?

That depends on what you enter. Record where things are held and the last 4 digits at most, never passwords, and keep your own backup. FamilyHub SG is in beta, so read the privacy policy first.
